What am I seeing? ＋

Cassini found water-ice grains and vapour venting from four warm fractures near Enceladus’s south pole, nicknamed the tiger stripes. Backlit by the Sun, the fine grains scatter light forward, so the plume glows most when you look toward the Sun.

Each grain here follows the same local Hill equations as the A-ring window: Saturn’s tide, the orbital rotation and Enceladus’s gravity, in units of its Hill radius (about 950 km) and its orbital rate (one unit ≈ 5.2 hours). Enceladus is 252 km in radius and its escape speed is about 239 m/s. Slower grains arc back down; faster grains leave its small Hill sphere and are sheared into a stream ahead of and behind the moon: the beginning of the E ring.

Vents are spread along four stripes about 130 km long and 35 km apart. The speed distribution, about one grain in six escaping at 1×, and the vertical exaggeration of grain sizes are illustrative; vapour, charging and drag from Saturn’s magnetosphere are omitted.
