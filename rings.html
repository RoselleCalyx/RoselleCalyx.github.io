What am I seeing? ＋

At this illustrative orbit, 130,000 km from Saturn’s centre, a revolution takes about 13.3 hours. The clock uses Saturn’s measured gravitational parameter; the default Hill length of 100 metres is an example, not a measured moonlet. The backdrop and visible grain sizes are composed for the view.

RH marks a characteristic gravity scale, not a hard wall. L₁ and L₂ are the nearby equilibrium points of this softened rotating-frame model. Paths are integrated test trajectories; the density layer shows a short rolling average of actual particle counts, relative to a uniform stream. For a selected grain, Δv measures departure from local circular shear; C is its dimensionless Jacobi integral, approximately conserved while the moonlet mass stays fixed and the grain remains in the window.

This window shares the moonlet’s orbit: inner grains overtake it while outer grains lag behind. The undisturbed streams still orbit Saturn in the same direction.

Ice follows different orbital speeds across the ring. A moonlet pulls on the passing grains, leaving a pair of disturbed streams. Try increasing its mass, then watch from above.

This is a simplified local gravity model: Saturn’s tidal field, orbital rotation and a moonlet’s gravity. Time is compressed. The moonlet body is 0.64 RH, corresponding to an illustrative bulk density near 700 kg/m³ at this orbit. The central stream is simulated; distant ice supplies context. Grain-to-grain collisions and self-gravity are omitted; this is not a reconstruction of a particular Cassini observation.
