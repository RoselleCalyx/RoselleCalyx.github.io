# Farm motion — 2026-10-09

## Assessment and implementation

The original six-frame sheets are useful for compact picture-book animation. The cat's similar poses, a fixed playback timer unrelated to travel, instant standing/sitting swaps and a scale-through-zero direction transition made its movement feel stiff or slippery. Increasing the frame count alone does not solve those runtime issues.

The revised cat has eight distinct frames in `assets/farm/walk/snowcat-v2.webp` (3072 × 384, alpha). Original walk sheets remain available. All eight species now use distance-driven frames in `js/farm-motion.js`, gradual acceleration/braking, stationary preparation before a direction change, and mostly lateral roaming routes. There is no extra whole-image bounce while a walk sheet is shown. Frame selection follows actual ground distance, including pond detours; it freezes while held or in calm/reduced-motion mode. This is stylized sprite animation, not a skeletal or biomechanical simulation; six frames remain the main limitation for the other seven walking species.

New assets in `assets/farm/poses/`: snowcat stand, sleep, stretch, sniff; rabbit, panda, fox, shiba, hedgehog, duckling and penguin sleep/rest. Cat waking proceeds stretch → stand → walk; arrival briefly stands before another rest decision. Petting triggers a stretch for the cat and a seated response for other species. Loaded poses cross-fade over 180 ms without changing the actor's layout. Missing or undecoded assets keep the original sitting image visible.

Runtime exports use 384-pixel squares with contact baseline 360/384. Generated figures were isolated as complete alpha components before packing; simply slicing the generated grid had clipped noses and carried neighbouring tail fragments into cells. Export uses one common scale for cat walk frames and another common scale for its posture group, retaining natural low silhouettes for sleep/stretch. The original other-species baselines stay at 353/384 with a compensating visual offset.

The existing painted backgrounds and seasonal tree simulation are retained. A shared gentle wind now drives tree sway, weather drift and sparse foreground grass, with each tree having slightly different flexibility and its roots fixed. Grass and water ripples are hidden during snowy winter. This preserves the illustrated style while adding local motion. The background painting and animal anatomy are not regenerated every frame.

